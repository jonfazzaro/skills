# Sources and scope

Verified on 20 November 2026. This skill applies selected, publicly accessible teaching to draft editing. It does not reconstruct the book, reproduce its language, or promise to recreate the author's literary voice.

## Book excerpts

- [Verlyn Klinkenborg on Writing More Clearly, Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/verlyn-klinkenborg-on-writing-more-clearly/), 22 July 2022. An attributed excerpt from *Several Short Sentences About Writing*. Supports attention to literal meaning and implication, short sentences as a starting practice, audible variation in length, and eventual use of longer sentences. It provides no numeric formula used by this skill.
- [Publisher's excerpt, Penguin Random House](https://penguinrandomhousehighereducation.com/book/?isbn=9780307958495). Supports continual sentence revision, precise naming, and attention to rhythm and movement.
- [Writing, one bit at a time, Geist](https://www.geist.com/for-writers/writing-one-bit-at-a-time). Publishes a brief excerpt illustrating experimental word removal: evaluate what disappears or improves rather than assuming every deletion helps.

## Author interviews

- [A Conversation with Verlyn Klinkenborg on the Craft of Academic Writing, Marginalia](https://www.marginaliareviewofbooks.com/post/a-conversation-with-verlyn-klinkenborg-on-the-craft-of-academic-writing), 5 March 2013. In the revision discussion, Klinkenborg recommends displaying sentences separately to inspect length and structural repetition. He also discusses unnecessary transitions, ambiguous connecting words, and the weight of sentence endings. The final-paragraph policy in this skill is our editorial adaptation of that inspection method.
- [Interview with Daniel Scrivner](https://www.danielscrivner.com/18/), transcript around 38:21–46:03. Klinkenborg discusses rhythmic possibilities within and between short sentences, returning to longer constructions, and reading aloud to recover sensitivity to rhythm.

## Access limitation and editorial additions

The [Internet Archive edition supplied by the user](https://archive.org/details/isbn_9780307266347) was marked access-restricted. Its full text was not consulted. Do not claim complete coverage or invent page references. Consult user-supplied passages when a request depends on a teaching absent from these sources.

Preserving a draft's evidence and voice, restoring connected paragraphs, rejecting manufactured drama, and avoiding length quotas are operating choices for this skill. They implement the user's brief; they are not attributed quotations or a universal theory of prose.
