# Editorial examples

These examples are original illustrations of the skill's decisions. They are not quotations from Klinkenborg or templates to reproduce.

## Short sentences belong inside passages

Draft:

> Because the loading door had swollen in the rain, which had continued throughout the night, we had to carry the chairs through the kitchen, and this meant that setting up the hall took longer than we had expected, although we still finished before the first guests arrived.

Edit:

> Rain had swollen the loading door overnight. We carried the chairs through the kitchen, which took longer than we expected. The hall was ready before the first guests arrived.

The edit separates the obstacle, response, and result. All three belong in the same paragraph. The last sentence reports an outcome without elevating it into a lesson.

Failure:

> The door wouldn't open.
>
> So we found another way.
>
> That's what commitment looks like.

This loses the practical details, adds a moral, and substitutes dramatic spacing for development.

## Joining can restore proportion

Draft:

> Mara checked the gauge. It had not moved. She waited beside the pump. She waited for ten minutes. Then she called the caretaker.

Edit:

> Mara checked the gauge. It had not moved. After waiting beside the pump for ten minutes, she called the caretaker.

The two brief opening sentences establish what Mara saw. Joining the remaining actions removes redundant starts and lets the sequence proceed. Another context could justify a different arrangement; this is not a prescribed cadence.

## Keep a longer sentence that works

Draft:

> From the back step I could see the washed cups on the kitchen shelf, the towel hanging from the oven door, and my brother asleep at the table with his shoes still on.

Decision: keep it. The sentence moves through a single field of view, and its last detail changes the scene. Splitting at each comma would give the objects equal, repetitive emphasis.

## Do not spend uncertainty to buy brevity

Draft:

> In the small pilot, the new form appeared to reduce incomplete applications, although staff also offered applicants more help during the same period.

Possible edit:

> The new form appeared to reduce incomplete applications in the small pilot. Staff also offered applicants more help during that period, so the form's contribution is uncertain.

The edit makes the existing qualification explicit. It would be wrong to write that the form reduced errors or proved its value. In a light edit, retaining the original would also be reasonable.

## Keep a necessary connection

Draft:

> The switch closes only while the cover is latched. If the cover opens, the motor stops.

Decision: keep the condition. Removing “only while” or “if” would change how the mechanism works. Concision cannot justify that loss.
